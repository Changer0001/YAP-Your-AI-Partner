Istanbul is the largest city in Turkey, with over 15 million residents—about 18% of the country’s population. 

Located on both Europe and Asia, it straddles the Bosphorus Strait and serves as Turkey’s economic, cultural, and historical hub.

Founded as Byzantium around 657 BCE by Greek settlers, 

it was later renamed Constantinople in 330 CE by Roman Emperor Constantine.

 The city served as the capital of the Roman, Byzantine, Latin, and Ottoman Empires.
 
 In 1453, the Ottomans took control and turned it into a major Islamic center. 

After the Republic of Turkey was established in 1923, the capital moved to Ankara, but Istanbul remained a key global city.

Today, Istanbul is a major international tourism destination, a transportation and industrial hub, and a top science and technology center. 

It is home to world-famous landmarks, universities, sports clubs, and two international airports. 

The name “Istanbul” comes from the Greek phrase eis tèn pólin, meaning “to the city,” and became official in the 20th century.
